# NYC DOT FOIL request — Brooklyn Bridge record drawings

Everything needed to file, plus what to do with the response. The request text is in §3 and is
meant to be pasted with only the bracketed identity fields changed.

Verified against [NYC DOT's FOIL page](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/about/foil.shtml).

## 2. Before filing — the agency asks you to check what is already public

Doing this is not just courtesy; a request for something already published invites a brush-off that
costs weeks. Already checked and *not* sufficient:

- **NYC DOT Bridges and Tunnels Annual Condition Report** — condition ratings, no geometry. The
  2024 edition lists this structure as **BIN 2240019**, inspected 11/27/2024, condition rating 4.85
  ("FAIR"). That is where the BIN in the request below comes from; it is worth quoting because it
  shows the request names the same structure the agency's own records do.
- **NYC Open Data** — no bridge structural drawings.
- **HAER No. NY-18** (already held, SRC-002/007) — one measured drawing sheet and 77 photographs.
  The photographs answer *arrangement*; only that one sheet carries dimensions.
- **The Brooklyn Bridge Promenade study, Aug 2016** (already held, SRC-011) — dimensioned promenade
  typologies, nothing on the towers, trusses or approaches.

Say so in the request. It demonstrates the records are not already available and narrows what you
are asking for.

## 3. Request text

> **Subject: FOIL request — Brooklyn Bridge structural record drawings and inspection reports**
>
> To the Records Access Officer:
>
> Under Article 6 of the New York State Public Officers Law, I request access to the following
> records concerning the **Brooklyn Bridge** (NYC DOT structure; **BIN 2240019**; spanning the East
> River between Park Row, Manhattan and Sands Street, Brooklyn).
>
> I request **electronic copies** where they exist, delivered by download link or electronic
> transfer. I do not require certified copies. Where a record exists only on paper or microfilm,
> please tell me its extent and the estimated copying cost before producing it, so I can narrow the
> request rather than incur an avoidable fee.
>
> **1. Record and as-built drawings of the main bridge**, including but not limited to:
> a. Tower elevations, plans and sections, including plan dimensions at the top of the towers and
>    at the deck, showing the taper between them.
> b. Details of the tower tops at the cable saddles: the masonry openings through which the main
>    cables pass, the saddle bearings and their supports, and the cornice.
> c. Typical and tower-line transverse sections through the deck, showing the lateral positions of
>    the four stiffening trusses, the truss depths, and the floor framing.
> d. The longitudinal profile of the roadway and of the promenade, with elevations referenced to a
>    stated datum.
>
> **2. Record and as-built drawings of the approach viaducts**, Manhattan and Brooklyn, showing the
> masonry arcades: bay spacing, pier dimensions, arch spans and rises, and the parapet.
>
> **3. Drawings of the promenade access stairs**, in particular the Brooklyn staircase descending
> to Washington Street and the Manhattan approach staircase: plans, sections and dimensions.
>
> **4. The two most recent biennial bridge inspection reports** for this structure, including any
> attached sketches, load ratings and element-level condition data.
>
> **5. Contract and record drawings from the most recent rehabilitation programme**, to the extent
> they document as-built geometry rather than temporary works.
>
> **6. Any index, drawing register or file list** covering the above. **If producing the drawings
> themselves is burdensome, I would value the index first** — it would let me identify a much
> smaller set of specific sheets and resubmit a narrower request.
>
> **Context, offered to help you locate the records rather than as part of the request:** I am
> building an openly published, source-documented three-dimensional model of the bridge, in which
> every dimension is traceable to a cited source and anything unsourced is labelled a placeholder.
> The records above would replace approximately twelve invented placeholder dimensions with
> documented ones. The project is public at
> https://github.com/Ethical-Tech-CoLab/brooklyn-bridge-3d and any records provided will be
> credited to NYC DOT.
>
> I have already reviewed and am **not** requesting: the NYC DOT Bridges and Tunnels Annual
> Condition Report; NYC Open Data holdings; HAER No. NY-18 at the Library of Congress; and the
> August 2016 Brooklyn Bridge Promenade study. Those are held already and do not contain the
> dimensions sought.
>
> If any part of this request is denied, please cite the specific statutory exemption for each
> withheld record and release the remainder, including any reasonably segregable portions.
>
> If security review is required before release of structural drawings, I am content to receive a
> redacted set, or to discuss narrowing the request to the elevations and sections that carry the
> dimensions rather than any security-sensitive detail.
>
> Please acknowledge receipt and provide a date certain for a response.
>
> [Name] · [Postal address] · [Email] · [Phone]

**Why it is shaped this way.** FOIL requires records be *reasonably described* — the commonest
denial is "the request is overbroad", not "these records are exempt". Every item names a structure,
a record type and a purpose. Item 6 is the escape hatch: a drawing index is cheap for the agency to
produce and lets a second, surgical request follow. Naming what you already hold pre-empts the
"already publicly available" response. Offering to accept redaction pre-empts the security
objection, which is the one genuinely likely to bite on a landmark bridge.

## 4. The statutory clock

| When | What must happen |
|---|---|
| **5 business days** from receipt | The agency must grant access, deny in writing, or acknowledge in writing with an approximate date |
| **20 business days** | If it acknowledged, that is the outside date for granting or denying; beyond it the agency must give a **date certain** and a reason |
| **Failure to meet the above** | Is a **constructive denial** — appealable exactly like a written one |
| **30 days** from a denial | Your window to appeal, in writing, to the Appeals Officers |
| **10 business days** from your appeal | The agency must fully explain in writing or grant access. Missing this is itself a denial (§89(4)(b)) and opens Article 78 review |

Diary the 5-day and 20-day dates when you file. Agencies respond to requesters who track dates.

If the appeal fails, the **NYS Committee on Open Government** issues free advisory opinions
(docs@dos.ny.gov). They carry no force but agencies take them seriously and it costs nothing.

## 4b. The appeal, ready to send

The likeliest outcome is not a refusal on the merits — it is **silence**, or a partial release that
omits the drawings. Both are appealable, and the appeal is a short letter. Statutory basis:
Public Officers Law §89(4)(a) gives you thirty days and gives the agency ten business days to
answer in full.

Send to **foilappeal@dot.nyc.gov** (Edalia George and Michael Twomey), copying
foiladmin@dot.nyc.gov, with the original request and any response attached.

> **Subject: FOIL appeal — [request number] — Brooklyn Bridge structural records**
>
> To the Records Access Appeals Officer:
>
> Under Public Officers Law §89(4)(a) I appeal the determination on my request of [date],
> reference [number], for record drawings and inspection reports for the Brooklyn Bridge
> (BIN 2240019).
>
> *[Use whichever applies.]*
>
> **If nothing was received:** The agency has neither granted access, denied the request in
> writing, nor furnished a written acknowledgement within five business days. Under §89(4)(a) and
> the Committee on Open Government's guidance, that failure constitutes a constructive denial, and
> I appeal it as such.
>
> **If the date has slipped:** The agency acknowledged the request on [date] with an approximate
> date of [date], which has now passed without a determination or a further date certain. I appeal
> that constructive denial.
>
> **If records were withheld:** The response withheld [describe] without citing a specific
> exemption for each withheld record. FOIL presumes access; the burden of justifying a withholding
> rests with the agency, and any reasonably segregable portion of an exempt record must still be
> released. I ask that the withholding be reversed or, at minimum, that each exemption be cited
> specifically and the segregable remainder produced.
>
> I remain willing to accept redacted drawings, or to narrow the request to the elevations and
> sections carrying dimensions, if security review is the obstacle. I would also accept the drawing
> index alone as a first step, which item 6 of the original request offered.
>
> Please respond within ten business days as §89(4)(a) requires. That subdivision also provides
> that the agency shall immediately forward a copy of this appeal, and of your determination on it,
> to the Committee on Open Government.
>
> [Name] · [Address] · [Email] · [Phone]

That last sentence matters more than its length suggests: the forwarding duty is the agency's, not
yours, and citing it signals that the deadline will be noticed by someone other than the requester.

**If the appeal is not determined within ten business days**, that failure is itself a denial, and
§89(4)(b) opens review by the courts under CPLR Article 78. That is a real step with real cost, so
in practice the sequence before it is: a polite reminder at the ten-day mark, then a free advisory
opinion from the Committee on Open Government (docs@dos.ny.gov), then Article 78 only if the
records genuinely matter more than the effort. For this project they probably do not — the
fallbacks in §6 are cheaper than litigation and often faster.

## 5. Processing the response — the part that matters

Records arriving is where a source-governed model is most at risk, because a drawing *feels*
authoritative and the temptation is to start typing numbers.

**Register before reading.** Give every document a `SRC-0xx` row in `SOURCE-REGISTER.md`, marked
**registered, not read**, before extracting a single dimension. Commit the file bytes and record a
SHA-256. A number whose source row was written afterwards is a number nobody can audit.

**Index the whole thing before using any of it.** This is the lesson that cost this project a
272-photograph campaign: SRC-007 was marked read on the strength of its data pages while its 77
photographs — which answered two of the open questions — went unindexed for the entire project.
Produce a `*-index.json` listing every sheet and what it depicts, the way
[sources/haer-ny18-photo-index.json](sources/haer-ny18-photo-index.json) does, **before** the first
control is edited.

**Grade honestly, per document, not per delivery.**

| Document | Grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| As-built / record drawing, dimension printed on it | `A` | An official measured statement |
| Same drawing, dimension *scaled off* it | `B`, and say so | Scaling is arithmetic on an image |
| Contract drawing marked "proposed" or unstamped | `B` at best | Intent is not as-built — see CONF-002, where a 1876 specification disagreed with the as-built cable |
| Inspection report, condition and element data | `A` for condition | It is an inspection, its purpose |
| Inspection report, dimensions | `B` unless dimensioned | Field notes are not survey |

**Check the datum before any elevation is used.** This model is entirely referenced to **mean high
water** and never converts. NYC DOT drawings may use NAVD88, the Borough of Manhattan datum, or a
project datum. An elevation adopted without checking its datum is a silent error of several feet
that every downstream check will pass. If the datum is unstated, the elevation is not usable —
record it and open an OQ rather than assuming.

**Expect conflicts and register them; do not overwrite.** Some drawings will disagree with SRC-002,
which is a grade `A` measured drawing. When they do, add a `CONF-0xx` row stating both values and
the reasoning, exactly as CONF-001 through CONF-009 do. A newer document is not automatically
right — it may be a proposal, a different datum, or a different measuring point.

**Change one control at a time and let the harness object.** After each edit run:

```powershell
python scripts\build_control_skeleton.py
python scripts\validate_dimensions.py
python scripts\prove_guards.py
```

`GRT-001`/`GRT-002` will fail until the model is rebuilt from the edited document — that is the
hash guard working. `CHK-001`–`CHK-008` are the interesting ones: a real dimension that breaks a
cross-source check has found either a mistake in the reading or a genuine disagreement, and both
are worth more than a clean run.

**Retire placeholders explicitly.** A `D` control becoming `A` should also close its open question
and update `OPEN-PROBLEMS.md`. Delete the placeholder row only after the OQ says why it went.

**Watch for the reverse failure.** A sourced dimension that never reaches geometry is invisible:
`CTL-046` registered eight saddle bearings at grade `A` and went unmodelled for the whole project.
`STT-015` now catches that class — keep it fed as new controls land.

## 6. If the drawings do not come

The likeliest outcomes are a security-based denial or a partial release. Fallbacks, in order:

1. **Ask for the index alone** (item 6). Rarely refused, and it makes the next request precise.
2. **NYC Municipal Archives** — holds Department of Bridges records; a different custodian with a
   different disposition, and archival material attracts fewer security objections.
3. **NYS DOT** — holds biennial inspection records for all NYS bridges including city-owned ones.
4. **Columbia University's Roebling collections and the Brooklyn Historical Society** — original
   construction drawings, which answer the 1883 geometry even if not the current condition.
5. **Commission a survey.** Retires class B problems *and* class A #4 at once, and is the only
   route to `MEASURED` geometry.
